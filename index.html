Two stories in Genesis are built like mirrors. The second half retells the first in reverse order, and the turning point sits at the exact centre. In both stories, that centre is God acting.

The centre

In Hebrew, “remember” means to act on someone's behalf. Right after 8:1 a wind passes over the earth and the waters begin to fall, an echo of the spirit over the waters in Genesis 1:2. Before this verse the world is being undone. After it, creation starts again: dry land, birds and animals, and the blessing “be fruitful and multiply” (9:1).

How exact is the count?

The verse divisions come from the Masoretes, the scribes who fixed the Hebrew text, and the numbers were added in the Middle Ages. So the 38 + 1 + 38 split may reflect how the text was divided rather than the original author. The scenes mirror each other either way. Wenham's own outline is framed by Noah's sons (6:10 and 9:18–19), a little wider than this count, and the scene boundaries here are simplified to fit the 77 verses. Genesis already knows the number: Lamech boasts of vengeance “seventy-sevenfold” (4:24).

Babel · wordplay

Mirrors in the sound

Some of the symmetry only exists in Hebrew. The narrator picks words whose letters and sounds answer each other.

Their bricks, reversed

The builders say nilbenah, “let us make bricks”, from the letters L‑B‑N. God answers navlah, “let us confuse”, from N‑B‑L. Same three letters, in reverse order. Hebrew reads right to left.

Babel and confusion

  • בָּבֶלBavel · Babylonv.9
  • בָּלַלbalal · to confusev.9

In Babylonian the name meant Bab‑ili, “gate of the gods”. Genesis explains it with balal, so the city's proudest boast becomes a pun on confusion.

Babel · measure for measure

Echoes across the story

Two of the builders' own phrases come back later, fulfilled in a way they never intended.

Their fear comes true

v.4 · the builders פֶּן־נָפוּץ עַל־פְּנֵי כָל־הָאָרֶץ “…lest we be scattered over the face of the whole earth.”
↓ four verses later
v.8 · the narrator וַיָּפֶץ יְהוָה אֹתָם מִשָּׁם עַל־פְּנֵי כָל־הָאָרֶץ “So the LORD scattered them from there over the face of the whole earth.”

The tower was built to prevent this, and the narrator describes the outcome in the builders' own words. The rabbis called this midah keneged midah, measure for measure.

They get a name

v.4 · the builders וְנַעֲשֶׂה־לָּנוּ שֵׁם “Let us make a name for ourselves.”
↓ the last verse
v.9 · the narrator עַל־כֵּן קָרָא שְׁמָהּ בָּבֶל “Therefore its name was called Babel.”

They wanted fame and the city is remembered as “Confusion”. The next passage is the family line of Shem, whose name means “name”, leading to Abraham, who hears: “I will make your name great” (12:2).

Babel · a second layer

Two patterns at once

Read straight through, the two halves also run in parallel. The builders act in vv. 1–4, and God answers point for point in vv. 6–9. The story mirrors itself and repeats itself at the same time.
